Frequently asked questions

What is the conveyancing process?

Conveyancing is the legal process of transferring property ownership from one party to another. It has seven stages, and each one matters:

  1. Property information and title review A detailed look at the title and legal documents to confirm ownership and spot problems early.
  2. Property searches Searches with the local authority and other bodies: planning, roads, drainage, environmental risks.
  3. Mortgage conveyancing Working with your lender on the legal side of the mortgage offer and its registration.
  4. Signing contracts Contracts prepared and checked so every term is clear before you sign.
  5. Exchanging contracts The moment it becomes legally binding for buyer and seller. The completion date is fixed here.
  6. Completion Funds move, ownership passes and you collect the keys.
  7. Post-completion Stamp Duty paid where due and the property registered with HM Land Registry.

How long does conveyancing take?

Typically 8 to 12 weeks from the time an offer is accepted until completion. It can vary with:

  • How complex it is. Leasehold properties or titles with unresolved issues may take longer.
  • Searches and enquiries. Delays can happen if more searches or answers are needed.
  • The mortgage. Getting and processing a mortgage offer affects the timeline.
  • The chain. If another sale in the chain is delayed, your completion date may move.

We keep you informed at every stage and work to deal with problems as soon as they appear.

How can a conveyancer help me?
  • Legal documents: drafting, reviewing and managing contracts.
  • Property searches: finding issues such as planning restrictions or disputes.
  • Advice: explaining legal terms and answering your questions throughout.
  • Funds: moving deposits, mortgage money and final payments securely.
  • Third parties: dealing with lenders, estate agents and the other side’s lawyers for you.
  • Registration: registering your ownership with HM Land Registry after completion.

Must I use a local solicitor for conveyancing?

No. Most conveyancing can be handled remotely, by email, phone and online, so you can work with a lawyer anywhere in England and Wales, or from abroad. What matters most is the lawyer’s experience with your kind of transaction.
